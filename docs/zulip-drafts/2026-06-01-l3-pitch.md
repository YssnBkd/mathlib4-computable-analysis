**Title:** *L3 keystone of a P-R-style computable-analysis formalization
compiles — design feedback on `ComputabilityStructure` typeclass*

---

Following up on the [`#new contributors` thread](<L0-thread-URL>) about a
Lean 4 / Mathlib4 formalization of Pour-El & Richards-style computable
analysis: the **L3 architectural keystone now compiles**. Source at
[github.com/.../formal/ComputableAnalysis/L3/ComputabilityStructure.lean](https://github.com/...)
— a `class ComputabilityStructure` parameterized over `(𝕜 : Type*) [RCLike 𝕜]
[ScalarComputableSeq 𝕜] (E : Type*) [NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace 𝕜 E]
[CompleteSpace E]`, with five fields: the primitive `IsComputableSeq :
(ℕ → E) → Prop`, the three P-R Ch. 2 §1 axioms (Linear Forms / effective
Limits / Norms), and a non-vacuity field `zero_seq`. Axiom 3 reaches down to
L1's `IsComputableSeqReal`. Plus two derived defs: `IsComputableElement
(x : E)` (constant sequence is computable) and `IsComputableDoubleSeq`
(`Nat.unpair`-reindexed). Zero `sorry`s in the L3 file; one auxiliary
typeclass `ScalarComputableSeq 𝕜` with the canonical `ℝ` instance via L1
(`ℂ` instance deferred to whenever `IsComputableSeqComplex` lands at L1).

The **headline design tension** we'd value pushback on is the P-R-vs-TTE
choice. P-R add a *predicate* on sequences-of-vectors-in-`E`, using Mathlib's
existing Banach-space typeclasses as substrate; the alternative TTE /
represented-spaces / Weihrauch line (Pauly–Brattka–Schröder) would build a
parallel category of computable Banach spaces with realizer maps as the
primitive. We chose P-R explicitly because adding *structure* to existing
Mathlib spaces avoids a second hierarchy — at the cost of one cross-layer
reach-down to L1 in Axiom 3 (norms-of-computable-sequence) and the
`ScalarComputableSeq` shim for the coefficient field. The L3 design doc
([`claims/l3-computability-structure/axioms.md`](https://github.com/...)) went
through 8 internal devil's-advocate rounds before the Lean encoding; the
typeclass field-shape is now stable, but the **conceptual** tradeoff has not
been adjudicated by anyone outside the project. If you've thought hard about
TTE-in-Mathlib (or about Bishop-style alternatives), we'd really like your
view on whether the P-R framing leaves money on the table for downstream
results.

Three specific Lean design questions where we'd appreciate Mathlib-house-style
input:

1. **`ScalarComputableSeq` auxiliary typeclass.** We made this a separate
   class because `IsComputableSeq` on a Banach space `E` needs a referent
   for "computable sequence of scalars in `𝕜`" in Axiom 1, but the L1
   definitions live on `ℝ` (and eventually `ℂ`) only — they're not
   `Computable`-derivable for general `[RCLike 𝕜]`. Is the auxiliary class
   the right shape, or would you prefer a bundled field directly on
   `ComputabilityStructure`?
2. **Double-sequence decoding via `Nat.unpair`.** Axioms 1 and 2 quantify
   over computable double sequences `ℕ × ℕ → α`, which we operationalize as
   `IsComputableSeq (fun n => f (Nat.unpair n))`. P-R commit existentially
   over "one of the standard recursive pairing functions"; we fix
   `Nat.pair`/`Nat.unpair`. Closure under recursive re-indexing (so
   different pairings give equivalent predicates) is a downstream lemma.
   Acceptable, or do you prefer a more `Function.uncurry`-shaped or
   `Equiv`-based parameterization?
3. **Effective separability NOT in the typeclass.** A bare
   `ComputabilityStructure` admits non-equivalent computability structures
   on the same Banach space (the trivial-zero predicate satisfies all three
   axioms vacuously); intrinsic uniqueness only kicks in via the L4
   Stability Lemma under an *effective separability* side-condition. We
   factored separability out as a downstream `[EffectivelySeparable …]`
   mixin rather than baking it into L3. This is the most ad-hoc choice in
   the design — comments welcome.
